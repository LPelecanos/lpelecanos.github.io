Distributed sensing · SHM · monitoring-informed mechanics

Turning continuous strain measurements into engineering understanding.

Distributed fibre-optic sensing (DFOS) and structural health monitoring (SHM) form a long-running strand of the research programme: instrument real infrastructure, recover spatially continuous response, interpret it using mechanics, and use the result to test or update computational models.

Research logic

From measurement to model to decision.

The value of sensing is not the data volume alone. The objective is to recover mechanisms, parameters and asset behaviour that conventional point measurements may miss.

01

Instrument

Design a sensing layout around the mechanics and the decision that matters.

02

Measure continuously

Use distributed strain or complementary dynamic sensing to observe response over space and time.

03

Interpret mechanics

Convert strain into curvature, load transfer, deformation or structural response.

04

Infer & update

Use inverse analysis, optimisation and model updating to estimate hidden parameters and capacity.

05

Support decisions

Translate measurements into assessment, validation, maintenance or design insight.

Field case studies

From underground infrastructure to foundations and structures.

Selected applications demonstrate the breadth of the sensing programme and its connection to major real assets.

CERN · Switzerland/France

TT10 concrete-lined tunnel

Long-term deformation monitoring using distributed fibre-optic sensing, targeting spatially continuous structural response over an operational underground asset.

DFOS · tunnel deformation · long-term monitoring

London Bridge Station · UK

Masonry arch under nearby piling

Distributed sensing used to observe the response of a masonry vault during adjacent construction activity and to understand deformation beyond isolated point gauges.

Historic masonry · construction interaction · SHM

Victoria & Albert Museum · London

Tension piles for deep basement works

Fibre-optic monitoring of tension piles, later supporting long-term interpretation of load transfer and pile performance in a major urban project.

Deep foundations · tension piles · long-term response

U.S. Embassy · London

Thermal pile response

Monitoring and thermo-mechanical interpretation of a thermal pile, linking temperature cycles, axial response and soil–pile interaction.

Energy geotechnics · thermal pile · thermo-mechanics

Farringdon · London

Osterberg-cell pile testing

Distributed fibre-optic monitoring of a working pile during O-cell testing, enabling high-resolution interpretation of axial load transfer along the shaft.

O-cell test · bored pile · load transfer

Geothermal foundations

Energy piles

Field monitoring and computational interpretation of thermo-hydro-mechanical behaviour in energy piles, extending sensing from static load tests to coupled thermal–mechanical response.

Geothermal · THM response · field monitoring

Structural sensing

Steel bridge I-beams

Distributed strain sensing applied to steel I-beam structural members, illustrating transfer of DFOS methods from geotechnical infrastructure to structural response.

Steel structures · distributed strain · bridge behaviour

Analysis & inference

Sensing becomes more powerful when coupled to mechanics.

The research has progressed from monitoring and interpretation towards inverse analysis: distributed strain can be used not only to observe response but to infer load-transfer behaviour, hidden parameters and even pile capacity.
