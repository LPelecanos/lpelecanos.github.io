Research programme

Mechanics-led research for resilient, monitored and lower-carbon infrastructure.

The programme connects computational geotechnics, structural dynamics, marine and offshore engineering, sensing and data-driven methods. The common objective is to understand infrastructure behaviour rigorously, validate models against physical evidence and translate that understanding into better engineering decisions.

Research pillars

Three mutually reinforcing programmes.

The emphasis is deliberately cross-disciplinary: ground, structure, fluid, sensing and computation are treated as interacting parts of infrastructure systems.

01 · Marine & offshore

Coastal, offshore and energy infrastructure

Hydrodynamic loading, offshore wind foundations, cyclic soil behaviour, buried offshore pipelines and fluid–soil–structure interaction.

  • Monopile gapping and long-term ratcheting
  • Wave and hydrodynamic loading
  • Buried offshore pipelines and large-deformation soil response
  • Waterfront and coastal infrastructure resilience

02 · Seismic & dynamic

Earthquake engineering and structural dynamics

Nonlinear response, system interaction, monitoring and model updating for critical infrastructure.

  • Dams and dam–reservoir–foundation interaction
  • Buildings, bridges and critical infrastructure
  • Dynamic soil–structure interaction
  • Time-history analysis and monitoring-informed calibration

03 · Smart & sustainable

Sensing, optimisation and data-enabled mechanics

High-resolution monitoring and computational inference for lower-carbon, better understood infrastructure systems.

  • Distributed fibre-optic sensing and structural health monitoring
  • Inverse analysis and PDE-constrained optimisation
  • Low-carbon foundation and structural design
  • AI/ML and physics-informed computational methods

Evidence

Published foundations for the programme.

Representative outputs show continuity from fundamental mechanics through field sensing to offshore and low-carbon applications.

Géotechnique · 2024

Monopile gapping and offshore wind turbine dynamics

A compact mechanics-based model of nonlinear pile–soil separation for dynamic offshore response.

Ocean Engineering · 2022

Long-term monopile ratcheting

A Winkler framework for accumulated cyclic deformation under repeated dynamic loading.

Computers & Geotechnics · 2013

Hydrodynamic pressures on dams

Computational treatment of fluid–structure interaction and hydrodynamic loading.

ASCE JGGE / C&G · 2018–2026

DFOS and inverse analysis of piles

Field sensing and computation used to infer load transfer, response and capacity.

Translation

From numerical models to monitored infrastructure.

A central aim is to close the loop between modelling, measurements and decisions.

Field monitoring

Distributed sensing, ambient vibration measurements and long-term monitoring of piles, tunnels, buildings and bridges.

Engineering practice

Research questions are framed around real design, assessment, maintenance and resilience decisions.

Standards & professional service

Participation in European structural-assessment standards and international dam-engineering activity connects research to wider engineering practice.

Education & supervision

Research-led teaching and doctoral supervision across computational mechanics, geotechnics, structural dynamics, offshore engineering and optimisation.

Forward programme

Priority directions for the next phase.

These directions extend established capability rather than creating disconnected new themes.

Marine infrastructure under multi-hazard loading

Coupled wave, current, seismic and soil-response modelling for coastal and offshore assets.

Monitoring-informed digital models

Structural health monitoring, system identification and model updating for bridges, buildings and marine infrastructure.

Offshore energy systems

Long-term nonlinear soil–foundation interaction, reliability and serviceability of offshore wind and related energy infrastructure.

AI-enabled mechanics

Surrogate, inverse and physics-informed models that remain anchored to conservation laws, constitutive behaviour and engineering interpretation.
