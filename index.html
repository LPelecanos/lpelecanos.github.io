About

Computational civil engineering across ground, structures and infrastructure.

Loizos Pelecanos is a civil engineer and academic researcher specialising in computational mechanics and dynamics. He is an Honorary Lecturer in the Department of Architecture & Civil Engineering at the University of Bath.

He studied Civil Engineering at Imperial College London, where he later completed a PhD in Computational Earthquake Engineering. He subsequently worked at the University of Cambridge Centre for Smart Infrastructure and Construction, combining advanced finite-element analysis with distributed fibre-optic sensing and field monitoring.

His research now connects nonlinear numerical modelling, experimental and field evidence, optimisation and data-driven methods across geotechnical, structural, offshore and marine engineering.

Research

Six connected research themes.

From fundamental mechanics to monitoring, model updating and AI-enabled engineering.

01

Computational Geotechnics

Nonlinear finite-element analysis of soils, foundations, piles, dams and complex soil–structure interaction.

02

Earthquake Engineering

Dynamic response of dams, buildings and infrastructure; nonlinear time-history analysis; seismic assessment and model validation.

03

Offshore & Marine Infrastructure

Monopile foundations, cyclic soil response, wave–structure interaction, buried pipelines and computational marine engineering.

04

Monitoring & Sensing

Distributed fibre-optic sensing, structural health monitoring, ambient vibration testing, inverse analysis and model updating.

05

Optimisation & Net Zero

Structural and foundation design optimisation, embodied carbon, reliability and computational routes to lower-carbon infrastructure.

06

AI/ML for Engineering

Surrogate models, inverse problems, physics-informed approaches and machine learning integrated with computational mechanics.
