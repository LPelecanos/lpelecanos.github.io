About

Computational engineering across ground, structures and infrastructure

Loizos Pelecanos is an Honorary Lecturer in the Department of Architecture & Civil Engineering at the University of Bath. His work combines numerical modelling, dynamics, sensing, optimisation and data-driven methods to understand and improve the performance of civil infrastructure.

He graduated from Imperial College London with an MEng in Civil Engineering and a PhD in Computational Earthquake Engineering, before conducting postdoctoral research at the University of Cambridge Centre for Smart Infrastructure and Construction.

His current research interests span geotechnical and structural earthquake engineering, nonlinear soil–structure interaction, offshore and marine infrastructure, monitoring and sensing, low-carbon foundations, and AI/ML-enabled computational engineering.

Research

Core themes

Computational Geotechnics

Finite-element modelling, nonlinear soil behaviour, piles, foundations, dam engineering and advanced soil–structure interaction.

Earthquake Engineering

Seismic response of dams, buildings and infrastructure, dynamic interaction, nonlinear time-history analysis and response prediction.

Offshore & Marine Infrastructure

Offshore foundations, buried pipelines, wind-energy infrastructure, wave–structure interaction and computational marine engineering.

Monitoring & Sensing

Structural health monitoring, distributed fibre-optic sensing, field measurements, inverse analysis and model updating.

Optimisation & Sustainability

Engineering design optimisation, low-carbon foundations, reliability and computational methods for sustainable infrastructure.

AI/ML in Engineering

Surrogate modelling, data-driven prediction, inverse problems and machine learning integrated with computational mechanics.
