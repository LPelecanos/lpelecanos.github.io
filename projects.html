Selected research projects

Mechanics-led projects with real infrastructure questions.

These programmes illustrate the breadth of the research while showing a consistent method: combine physics-based modelling with measurements, optimisation or uncertainty analysis to answer engineering questions that matter in design and assessment.

Offshore wind

Long-term nonlinear dynamics of monopile foundations

Question: How do cyclic soil response, gapping and accumulated deformation alter long-term offshore wind turbine dynamics?

Approach: Reduced-order Winkler-type models, nonlinear soil springs, cyclic constitutive rules and dynamic structural analysis.

Outcome: Mechanics-based formulations published in Ocean Engineering and Géotechnique, with a platform for multi-directional and combined-loading extensions.

Marine hydrodynamics

Hydrodynamic loading and fluid–structure interaction

Question: How should water-induced dynamic pressures be represented accurately without unnecessary computational complexity?

Approach: Finite-element hydrodynamics, analytical benchmarking and dynamic interaction models.

Outcome: A foundation for current work on coastal and marine structures, wave loading and coupled fluid–structure response.

Smart geotechnics

DFOS, piles and inverse analysis

Question: How can distributed strain measurements be converted into useful information about load transfer and pile capacity?

Approach: High-resolution fibre-optic sensing, mechanics-based interpretation, inverse analysis and PDE-constrained optimisation.

Outcome: Field-informed methods spanning O-cell testing, axially loaded piles, eccentric loading and capacity inference.

Seismic infrastructure

Monitoring and model updating of public infrastructure

Question: How can permanent sensing improve understanding of the dynamic condition and seismic behaviour of existing public assets?

Approach: Ambient response, modal identification, FE correlation, time-history analysis and progressively higher-grade permanent instrumentation.

Outcome: A national pilot programme for monitored public buildings and bridges in Cyprus.

Net zero foundations

Embodied-carbon optimisation of pile systems

Question: How far can foundation carbon and cost be reduced while preserving engineering performance?

Approach: Multi-level modelling, finite-element analysis, optimisation algorithms and material/system comparison.

Outcome: A sequence of published studies on lower-carbon pile design and construction decision-making.

Structural optimisation

Bridge and truss design optimisation

Question: How should structural size, geometry and dynamic performance be optimised under realistic engineering constraints?

Approach: Numerical optimisation, benchmark trusses, stability constraints, modal performance and interpretable computational workflows.

Outcome: A platform for static, dynamic and AI-assisted structural design optimisation.

Coastal geohazards · particle methods

Submerged granular landslides and landslide-generated waves

Question: How does momentum transfer from a deforming granular mass generate and redirect waves as the failure becomes progressively submerged?

Computational lineage: Lu Tong’s PhD developed DEM, SPH and coupled SPH–DEM tools for dynamic granular and saturated soils.

Current direction: Coupled particle modelling is being extended and independently verified for landslide-generated waves across partially to fully submerged conditions. Scour and sediment–structure interaction are prospective follow-on applications requiring dedicated validation.
