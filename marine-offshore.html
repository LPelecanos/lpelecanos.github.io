Marine & offshore infrastructure

From hydrodynamic loading to long-term soil–foundation response.

This research strand brings together fluid–structure interaction, nonlinear soil behaviour, offshore foundation dynamics, buried infrastructure and monitoring. The programme is mechanics-led, computationally rigorous and increasingly focused on resilient marine and coastal infrastructure.

Research continuity

A coherent marine/offshore trajectory.

The programme extends established work in hydrodynamics, geotechnical dynamics and field-informed modelling rather than creating a disconnected new theme.

Current research direction

Core capabilities

Four strands, one mechanics framework.

Fluid, soil, structure and data are treated as interacting components of the same infrastructure system.

Wave & hydrodynamic loading

Deterministic and irregular-wave loading, hydrodynamic pressures and coupled response of marine structures.

Nonlinear soil–foundation interaction

Gapping, ratcheting, cyclic degradation and nonlinear p–y-type response under long-term dynamic loading.

Buried offshore pipelines

Soil–pipeline interaction, uplift/lateral response and large-deformation computational modelling.

Monitoring-informed marine systems

Transfer of sensing, modal identification and inverse-analysis methods to marine and coastal infrastructure.

Forward programme

Priority directions in marine infrastructure.

A focused programme that connects coastal engineering, offshore energy, geotechnical dynamics and smart infrastructure.

Wave–soil–structure interaction

Coupled hydrodynamic and nonlinear foundation response for fixed offshore and coastal structures.

Multi-hazard marine infrastructure

Combined wave, current, seismic and long-term cyclic loading, with performance and resilience metrics.

Ports, quay walls & waterfront assets

Mechanics-based assessment, fragility and retrofit strategies for critical coastal infrastructure.

Monitoring-informed digital models

Modal identification, sensing and inverse analysis for condition assessment and model updating.

Offshore energy foundations

Long-term serviceability, reliability and soil–foundation interaction of monopiles and related foundation systems.

Physics-guided AI

Surrogate and inverse models that accelerate simulation while preserving mechanics and interpretability.

Research proposition

A bridge between coastal loading, geotechnical mechanics and infrastructure intelligence.

The distinctive opportunity is not to separate marine engineering from geotechnics or structural dynamics, but to integrate them: hydrodynamic loads, nonlinear foundation response, structural behaviour, monitoring and uncertainty within one computational framework.

Monitoring transfer

Marine infrastructure can be both modelled and measured.

The established DFOS/SHM capability provides a natural route to monitoring-informed marine engineering: piled jetties, quay walls, coastal bridges, offshore foundations and buried infrastructure can be linked to computational models through distributed and dynamic sensing.
